Quick Settings Checklist

  • Is your APN set correctly? An incorrect or missing APN is the most common cause of a connection that won't work at all.
  • Is Band Lock set to "All" or "Auto"? If it's locked to a specific band that's no longer available in your area, your connection can disconnect entirely.
  • Is Network mode set to Auto or Manual? On Manual, if your selected carrier becomes unavailable, the connection can drop. On Auto, the device picks a network for you — but it doesn't always choose the strongest one. If you already know which carrier gives you the best signal at your address, Manual is usually the better choice.
Not sure where to find these settings? Follow the guide for your Nighthawk — APN, Band Locking, and Manual Network guides. Not sure where to find these settings? Follow the guide for your Orbi — APN and Manual Network guides.

Check Your Cell Signal First

Our service runs on the same cellular network as your phone. Check whether your phone also has weak or no signal in the same spot — if so, the issue is likely the local network rather than your device.

  1. Restart Your Device
    • Unplug the device or turn it off for 10 seconds.
    • Plug it back in or turn it on, wait for a full restart, then try connecting again.
  2. Improve Signal Strength & Placement
    • Place the hub near a window, ideally on a higher floor and as close as possible to the nearest known cell tower.
    • Avoid basements, cabinets, or metal objects that can block signal.
    • Try a few different spots — small changes in placement can meaningfully change signal strength, so trial and error is worth it.
    • Check the hub's LEDs or app for signal strength. Aim for at least 3/5 bars.
  3. Know About Network Issues
    • Cell tower maintenance may cause temporary outages or slowdowns.
    • Peak hours (evenings/weekends) can slow speeds due to high network use.
  4. Recheck the Settings Checklist Above
    • Confirm the APN, Band Lock, and Network mode settings against the checklist at the top of this guide.
    • Try switching to another major Canadian network provider for a stronger signal.
  5. Inspect the SIM Card
    • Turn off and unplug the device.
    • Remove the SIM card, clean it gently with a soft cloth, and reinsert it securely.
    • Turn the device back on and check the connection.
  6. Test Your Speed
    • Measure your internet speed using a speed test tool.
    • Test at different times (morning, afternoon, evening).
    • Try moving the hub to different spots in your home.
    Run Speed Test

Fixing Weak Signal in Specific Rooms

A good signal in the living room but nothing in the basement or a back bedroom usually comes down to coverage rather than a fault with your plan. How you fix it depends on which device you have.

Steps below cover both devices — to see only what applies to you.
Showing steps for your Nighthawk.
Showing steps for your Orbi.

Nighthawk: Work With a Single Portable Hub

The Nighthawk is one hub, so the fix is usually about where it sits — or how you extend its signal — rather than the device itself.

  • Move it to the most central room in the home rather than tucking it in the room you use most — a few metres can matter more than you'd expect.
  • Keep it up off the floor and away from large appliances, mirrors, and metal furniture, which all block signal.
  • If one room is consistently weak, connect to the hub's Wi-Fi from just outside that room instead of deep inside it, or bring the hub with you if you're mobile.
  • A 2x2 MIMO antenna aimed at your nearest cell tower can meaningfully widen the hub's effective range.
  • A third-party Wi-Fi extender or access point can rebroadcast the Nighthawk's signal into weaker rooms. If you go this route, set the Nighthawk's Wi-Fi to stay on at all times in its settings — an extender depends on that signal always being available, and the default power-saving timeout will disconnect it.
  • For a more permanent setup, connect the Nighthawk to a dedicated router over Ethernet and let that router handle Wi-Fi for the whole home. You can still unplug the Nighthawk and take it with you when you're on the go — it's built to be portable either way.

Orbi: Extend Coverage With a Satellite or Third-Party Access Point

Orbi's mesh system is built to add coverage room by room, which makes it the better option for larger homes or multiple floors. A third-party extender or access point works too, if you'd rather go that route.

  • An Orbi satellite unit relays the signal from your main router into rooms it doesn't reach well on its own.
  • Place the satellite roughly halfway between the main router and the weak room, not inside the weak room itself.
  • Pair it through the NETGEAR Orbi app's "Add Satellite" flow — it walks you through placement and confirms signal strength as you go.
  • Avoid placing satellites in basements or behind large appliances, for the same reasons that affect the main unit.
  • A third-party Wi-Fi extender or access point connected to the Orbi works just as well if you'd prefer not to add a satellite.
Download the Orbi App
More4Less doesn't sell or supply extenders, access points, or dedicated routers — any of the equipment above is sourced through a retailer of your choice.

If the Page Won't Load

  1. Confirm You're on the Right Network
    • You need to be connected to your device's own Wi-Fi network (or plugged into it directly) — not your home network from another provider, and not mobile data.
  2. Type the Address Directly
    • Enter 192.168.1.1 in the browser's address bar rather than searching for it, and try adding http:// in front if it still doesn't load.

    • Some browser extensions and privacy settings can block local network pages — a different browser or an incognito/private window rules this out quickly.

    • Power the device off for 10 seconds, then back on, and try loading the page again once it's fully restarted.

What Do the Lights (or Icons) on My Device Mean?

Nighthawk hotspots communicate status through an on-screen display rather than blinking lights, while the Orbi LBR20 uses a Power LED and a ring LED. Here's how to read each.

Steps below cover both devices — to see only what applies to you.
Showing steps for your Nighthawk.
Showing steps for your Orbi.

Nighthawk: Reading the Touchscreen

  • Signal bars at the top of the screen show cellular strength, the same idea as the bars on a phone. Fewer than 2 bars usually points to a placement issue rather than a fault — see Internet isn't working.
  • Network type icon (5G, 5G UC, LTE, etc.) shows which network you're connected to.
  • A warning triangle generally means limited connectivity — the device has found a network but can't reach the internet. Restarting the device and rechecking APN and network settings is the first thing to try.
  • Battery icon fills as the device charges, and shows a lightning bolt while plugged in.

Orbi LBR20: Power LED & Ring LED

  • Power LED (on the back, above the power button): on means the router has power. If it's off, check the outlet and adapter as covered in Router won't turn on.
  • Ring LED (on top): pulses slowly while the router is booting, resetting, or syncing with a satellite — normal for a minute or two, and it settles once a stable connection is found.
  • A fast, continuous flash on the ring LED — rather than a slow pulse — usually signals a fault: no SIM detected, or repeated failed connection attempts. Restarting the device and rechecking the SIM and APN settings resolves most cases.
  • If the ring LED stays in a non-standard state well after the router has finished booting, that usually points to a connectivity issue rather than a hardware fault — a restart followed by rechecking your APN and SIM (see Internet isn't working) resolves most cases.
For the full color-by-color reference, see the Orbi LBR20 LED guide.

Too Many Devices, or One Won't Connect

Both device types support a generous number of connections, but a few common issues can make a "connected" device behave like it isn't.

Steps below cover both devices — to see only what applies to you.
Showing steps for your Nighthawk.
Showing steps for your Orbi.

Check Your Device Limit

  • Nighthawk M6 models support up to 32 devices connected at once. Near that limit, some devices may fail to join or drop intermittently.
  • The Orbi LBR20 supports dozens of simultaneous connections — coverage, not device count, is the more common cause of a device failing to connect.
  • If you're nowhere near the limit, the usual culprits are a mistyped password, a device stuck trying the wrong band, or a client with an outdated Wi-Fi driver.

Fixes to Try

  1. Re-enter the password
    • Type it fresh rather than relying on a saved copy, especially if you changed it recently.
  2. Rule Out Signal
    • Move the struggling device closer to the hub or router temporarily to see if that's the issue.
  3. Restart the Client's Wi-Fi
    • Toggle Wi-Fi off and on on the device that's having trouble, not just the hotspot/router.
  4. Check the Band
    • By default, the Nighthawk only broadcasts 5GHz. Older or simpler smart-home devices often need 2.4GHz — turn it on in WiFi Settings if a device won't connect at all.
    • The Orbi LBR20 broadcasts a combined 2.4/5GHz network and steers devices automatically, but you can split the bands in Advanced settings if a specific device insists on the wrong one.
  5. Forget & Reconnect
    • Remove the saved network on the client device entirely, then reconnect from scratch.

Port Forwarding, VPN Access, or a Static IP

This mostly comes up if you're running something like a security camera system, a game server, or remote-access software that needs an open inbound port.

Steps below cover both devices — to see only what applies to you.
Showing steps for your Nighthawk.
Showing steps for your Orbi.

Orbi LBR20

  • Port forwarding lives in the web admin at 192.168.1.1, under Advanced > Setup > Port Forwarding / Port Triggering.
  • Set a reserved local IP for the target device first (Advanced > Setup > LAN Setup > Address Reservation) so the forwarding rule doesn't break the next time the device reconnects.
  • Whether an inbound forward actually works over the cellular connection depends on your carrier. Some carriers assign the router a private, carrier-side address (CGNAT), which blocks inbound connections no matter how the router itself is configured — this isn't something adjustable on our end, and it's the most common reason forwarding "doesn't work" despite correct setup.

Nighthawk

Mobile hotspots are almost always behind carrier-side CGNAT, so inbound port forwarding generally isn't possible on the Nighthawk regardless of its settings. If you need reliable inbound access, a VPN provider with a hosted/relay option, or a static-IP plan through us, are the usual workarounds — open a ticket and we can walk through what fits your use case.

Using a Dedicated Router

If you need fuller control over your network — beyond what port forwarding, VPN, or parental controls offer on the device itself — an external dedicated router paired in bridge mode is a solid option. Bridge mode has the Orbi or Nighthawk pass the cellular connection straight through, while your own router handles all the networking, routing, and advanced features.

Using a VPN

We generally recommend running a VPN at the device level — installed directly on each device that needs it — or through a dedicated router with its own VPN capabilities, rather than relying on the Orbi or Nighthawk for this. VPN support on both is limited, since their main focus is cellular connectivity rather than advanced networking. The Orbi LBR20 does include a basic router-level VPN client under Advanced > VPN Service, but for anything beyond occasional use, a device-level VPN app or a dedicated VPN-capable router will serve you better.

Battery Care & Charging Tips

This guide applies to Nighthawk hotspots, which use a removable battery. The Orbi LBR20 runs on wall power only and has no internal battery — a third-party backup battery (a small UPS) is a great option if you want internet to stay up through a power outage.

Getting the Most from Your Battery

  • Expect up to roughly 9 hours of continuous use on a full charge — less with heavy data use, weak signal, or the screen left on and bright.
  • It's normal for the device to run warm during fast charging or heavy use; avoid covering it or leaving it in direct sun while charging.
  • Storing it for weeks at a time? Aim for around 50% charge rather than fully charged or fully drained — lithium batteries age faster sitting at either extreme.
  • Shortening the screen's sleep timer in Settings makes a noticeable difference to battery life over a full day.

If the Battery Won't Charge, or Drains Fast

  1. Try a Different Cable & Adapter
    • Use a USB-C cable and a 3.0A-or-higher wall adapter — many charging issues turn out to be the cable or adapter rather than the device.
  2. Let It Cool Down
    • Charging pauses automatically above about 45°C (113°F) internal temperature as a safety measure. Move it somewhere cooler and give it 15–20 minutes before trying again.
  3. Check for a Firmware Update
    • See Update your device's firmware — occasionally a charging quirk is fixed in a later release.
  4. Consider the Battery's Age
    • If it's more than a year or two old, or visibly swollen, it likely just needs replacing. Open a ticket and we can point you to a compatible replacement.
